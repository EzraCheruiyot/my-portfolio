The one marketing number most businesses ignore

BY EZRA CHERUIYOT · NOV 2026

Ask a business owner how their marketing is going and they'll usually reach for likes, followers, or "engagement." Those feel like progress. But none of them tell you the thing you actually need to know: is this making money?

There's a single number that does. It's called cost per result — how much you spent to get one thing that matters. One lead. One signup. One sale. Take what you spent, divide it by how many results you got, and you have it.

Spent KES 10,000 on ads and got 40 leads? Your cost per lead is KES 250. That one number is worth more than every like you've ever collected, because it answers the only question that pays your rent: for every shilling I put in, what comes back?

Why likes lie

A post can get a thousand likes and sell nothing. A quiet ad seen by far fewer people can bring in ten paying customers. Likes measure attention. Cost per result measures business. They are not the same, and confusing them is how people stay busy and broke at the same time.

You can't improve what you refuse to measure. And you can't measure your way to growth by counting the wrong thing.

How to actually use it

Once you know your cost per result, three doors open:

1. You can compare. Facebook vs. TikTok, this offer vs. that one, this week vs. last. Whatever brings results cheapest wins more budget. No more guessing which channel "feels" better.

2. You can scale with confidence. If a customer is worth KES 2,000 to you and you can acquire one for KES 400, you don't have a marketing problem — you have a printing press. Spend more. The math already told you it's safe.

3. You can fix what's broken. A rising cost per result is an early warning long before your bank balance notices. It tells you the offer, the audience, or the creative needs attention — while you can still do something about it.

The uncomfortable part

Most businesses avoid this number because it's honest. It can tell you the campaign you're proud of is losing money. But that honesty is exactly the point. The goal of marketing was never to look good — it was to return more than it cost. Cost per result is how you prove it did.

Start simple. Pick one result that matters to your business. Track what you spend to get it. Watch that number for a month. You'll make sharper decisions in four weeks than most of your competitors will make all year.
